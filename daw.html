How to use The Audiobook Guy DAW

A full recording and editing workspace for narration and voice over, right in your browser. Your audio stays on your computer — nothing leaves it unless you export to Google Drive.

Projects

  • New Project asks for a name and where to keep it. Choose a folder on your computer and the DAW creates Project Name › Project Name.tagdaw (the project file and backup) plus an Audio Files folder holding every take and import.
  • Everything saves automatically as you work. To come back to a project, use Recent projects, Open Project Folder, or drag the project's folder onto the start screen.
  • Open with a double-click — install the DAW as an app (the Install the DAW button on the start screen, or the install icon at the right of Chrome or Edge's address bar). After that, double-clicking any .tagdaw file opens that project. The first time, the DAW asks you to OK its folder — choose Allow on every visit and it opens straight away from then on.
  • Audio plays straight from your project folder instead of being loaded into the browser, so even long projects use very little memory. Keep the project folder where it is (and plugged in, if it's on an external drive) while you work.
  • Clean Project lists audio files the timeline no longer uses (old takes, deleted imports) and removes them from the folder.
  • Backup downloads the whole project — project file and audio — as one .zip. Open Backup restores it anywhere.
  • Saving to a folder needs Chrome or Edge on a computer. Other browsers keep projects inside the browser instead — fine for quick work, but download a Backup of anything that matters.

Recording

  • Set your microphone and headphones in Mic & Output. Arm a track with its red ● button — the meter shows your input level. Aim for peaks well below the red.
  • Punch & Roll — stop at a mistake, place the cursor where you want to pick up, and press R (or Ctrl/Cmd + R, or Shift + Space). The pre-roll plays the lead-in so you can match your read, then recording starts at the cursor. Everything after that point on the armed track is replaced by the new take (Undo brings it back).
  • Record over selected item — click an item, press R, and the new take replaces just that item. Recording keeps going until you stop it, so you can carry on past the item's end.
  • Record over time selection — drag across the armed track to select a stretch (it can cover several items) and press R. Only that stretch is replaced; recording keeps going until you stop it.
  • Press Space, Esc or R to stop. Takes are written as WAV files into your project's Audio Files folder while you record.
  • Recording Settings Checker (next to Mic & Output) — record a short guided test (3 seconds of silence, about 25 seconds of reading, 3 more seconds of silence) and it measures your raw peaks, RMS and room tone against the recommended levels: peaks −12 to −6 dB (never above −3 dB), RMS −35 to −25 dB, room tone −60 dB or lower. It tells you whether to change your gain, your delivery or your room, and flags things like clipping, hum, rumble, a noise gate left on, plosives and room echo. You can also select any recording on the timeline and check that instead.
  • Every take keeps all of your audio — the pre-roll and anything after the end of the item or selection are recorded too. The new item sits exactly where you punched in, and you can Alt/Opt-drag inside it to slide the take, or drag its edges to reveal the extra audio, to blend it with the takes either side.

Editing

  • Select — drag across a track to make a time selection, then drag either edge of it to fine-tune. Click an item to select it; click an empty spot or press Esc to deselect.
  • Split (S) cuts selected items — or the selected track — at the cursor, with a tiny crossfade so the cut is seamless.
  • Delete (Delete, Backspace or Shift + D) removes the selection and leaves a gap; everything else stays exactly where it is.
  • Delete & Join (Shift + Delete, Alt/Opt + Delete or Alt/Opt + Shift + D) closes the gap so everything after slides up, and crossfades the two pieces either side. The cursor jumps to a second before the join so you can press Play and hear it.
  • Close Gaps (Alt/Opt + Shift + Space) slides the items on the selected tracks back to back so there's no silence between them, with a short crossfade at each join so there are no clicks.
  • Drag an item's top bar to move it, including onto another track. Drag its edges to trim, and the small squares at its top corners to fade in or out. Overlap two items on a track and they crossfade automatically. Alt/Opt-drag inside an item slides its audio while the item stays put — the audio loops round, so you can always slide it, even when the item is the full length of its take (a faint dotted line shows where the take starts over).
  • Ripple editing (Alt/Opt + P, or the Ripple switch) — when it's on, everything after an edit moves with it: Delete closes the gap, trimming the end of an item pulls or pushes what follows, moving an item carries the rest of the track along, and pasting or importing makes room. Turn it off to change one item without disturbing anything else.
  • Every edit is non-destructive — your original takes are never changed, and Undo goes all the way back.

Track effects & analysis

  • Open Effects (Alt/Opt + F, the FX button on a track, or right-click) to build an effects chain for a whole track. Use ＋ Add effect to add Noise Reduction, EQ, a Compressor, a Limiter or the ACX Audio Fixer — as many as you like, even two of the same — and the ✕ on an effect to remove it (Undo brings it back). Drag the ⋮⋮ handle to change the order: the sound flows from the top effect to the bottom one. Press ▾ (or click an empty part of an effect's title bar) to fold its settings away, and ↺ to reset an effect to its starting settings — a learned noise profile is kept.
  • Each effect has its own on/off switch, and Effects chain switches them all on or off at once (Alt/Opt + Shift + F) so you can hear before and after. Every new track comes with a ready-made chain — EQ (High-pass 80 Hz), Noise Reduction (press Learn to give it a profile), Compressor, Limiter (−3.5 dB) and the ACX Audio Fixer — with Effects chain switched off until you want it. Effects run whenever you play or export; your takes are never changed. A track with effects on shows an orange FX button — crossed out while the chain is switched off.
  • Noise Reduction — press Learn, then play a few seconds of just room tone (no voice) and stop. The DAW learns that noise and takes it out. Amount sets how much: 0% leaves the audio as it is, 100% is complete silence. Use the least that sounds clean — too much can make a voice sound watery.
  • EQ — start from a preset (High-pass 80 Hz, Male or Female voice, Harsh sibilance, Boxy space and more), then fine-tune each band. The curve shows what you're doing to the sound.
  • Compressor — Strength evens out loud and quiet moments and Boost raises the level afterwards.
  • Limiter — stops peaks going over the ceiling without distorting. The ACX −3.5 dB preset keeps you safely inside ACX's −3 dB peak limit.
  • ACX Audio Fixer — sits at the end of the chain and sets up the effects above it for ACX. Press Run ACX Audio Fixer and it listens to the whole track, finds the cleanest room tone and learns your noise from it, then sets the high-pass EQ, Noise Reduction, Compressor and Limiter so the track lands at about −20 dB RMS, peaks at −3.5 dB and a noise floor close to −85 dB. Any of those effects that are missing are added for you. It shows before-and-after numbers and everything it changed, and if your raw recording is outside the recommended levels it explains why that matters. It makes no sound of its own, and Undo puts everything back.
  • Audio Analysis shows the highest peak and the RMS as you play. Scan whole track checks the entire track (after effects and volume) and shows its peak, RMS and noise floor against the ACX requirements.

Moving around & viewing

  • Space plays and stops — stopping returns the cursor to where you pressed Play. Enter pauses right where you are.
  • [ and ] jump to the start and end of the project; Ctrl/Cmd + [ or ] hop to the nearest item edge. ← → nudge the cursor a little (hold Shift for bigger steps).
  • ↑ ↓ (or + / −, or scroll over the timeline) zoom in and out around the mouse; Shift + scroll (or a sideways swipe) moves along the timeline; F fits the whole project.
  • Make a track taller — drag the bottom edge of the track (in its header or on the timeline) to see its waveform bigger. Double-click the edge to reset it. This only changes the view, never the volume.
  • Shift + ↑ ↓ (or the ↥ ↧ buttons) makes the waveforms taller or shorter so quiet bits are easier to see — again, display only.

Importing & exporting

  • Import WAV, MP3, FLAC, M4A, OGG and more with Import Audio, or drop files onto the timeline. WAV files are copied into the project as they are; other formats are converted to WAV so they can play straight from the folder.
  • Export mixes the whole project or the time selection to WAV, MP3 or FLAC — choose mono or stereo, sample rate, bit depth or MP3 bitrate. Download it, or upload it to Google Drive and get a shareable link straight away. Google sign-in is only asked for when you use Drive.
  • From the Audio Recorder, use Open in DAW to carry a quick recording over into a full project.

Recording Settings Checker

Before you record a whole chapter, check your mic, gain and room. Record a short test and the checker tells you what to adjust. It always measures your raw recording, with effects off.

Peaks−12 to −6 dB · never above −3 dB

The loudest moments of your recording. Too high and they clip — distortion that can't be removed. Too low and you'll need a big boost later, which brings the background noise up with it. Peaks are mostly set by your gain.

RMS−35 to −25 dB

Your average level — how loud you are overall. If your peaks are right but your RMS is low, the answer is your delivery, not more gain.

Room Tone−60 dB or lower

The sound of your space while you're silent: fans, traffic, hiss from your gear. The quieter it is, the less noise reduction you'll need and the more natural your voice will sound.

Your test recording
  1. Set your mic and headphones in Mic & Output.
  2. Press Record a test. Stay completely silent for 3 seconds — that's your room tone.
  3. When the prompt next to the time display says read now, read for about 25 seconds at your normal narration level.
  4. Stay silent for 3 more seconds. The test stops by itself and is checked straight away.

The test is recorded at the end of your project on the armed track, so nothing you've recorded is touched. Delete it afterwards if you don't need it.
